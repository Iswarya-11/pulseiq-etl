Attendance & Leave Analysis — Insights

=>Finding 1: Attendance varies across months
Monthly attendance ranged from 92.64% in May 2026 to 95.35% in July 2025. Attendance fell to 92.64% in May 2026 and improved to 95.00% in September 2026.

Meaning: Attendance changes over time, but the available data alone does not establish the reasons or prove a seasonal pattern.

HR action: Review monthly staffing, approved leave, holidays and team workload to understand the lower-attendance months.

=>Finding 2: Finance & Accounts has the lowest department attendance
Finance & Accounts recorded **90.60%** attendance, the lowest among the departments. Data & AI recorded the highest at 94.84%.

Meaning: The difference may reflect different work schedules, staffing levels or leave patterns; it does not automatically indicate an employee performance issue.

HR action: Review department-level schedules and leave trends with managers, and identify whether additional staffing support is needed.

=>Finding 3: AI-flagged attendance anomalies increased in some months
The anomaly dataset contains 221 flagged records. July 2026 had the highest monthly count, with 22 anomalies.

Meaning: These are records flagged by the sample anomaly data, not confirmed attendance violations or proven financial losses.

HR action: Ask HR to validate flagged records against attendance logs and approved leave before taking action. Investigate repeated patterns only after verification.

Data limitations and uncertainties
- The dataset may be synthetic or sample data and may not represent actual workforce behaviour.
- The available period covers April 2025 to September 2026; this is insufficient to confirm recurring annual or festival-related seasonality.
- Raw late-arrival and anomaly counts can vary with employee numbers and scheduled working days.
- An anomaly flag is a signal for review, not proof of misconduct.
- Department-level differences should be interpreted alongside staffing, schedules and leave approvals.
